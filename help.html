AI-Hab Identify

Use AI-Hab to quickly identify habitats from photos. The app provides AI-powered suggestions based on the UK Habitat Classification (UKHab), helping you record consistent, high-quality observations.

Quick start

  1. Tap Take a photo or Upload a photo.
  2. Wait a few seconds while the AI analyses your image.
  3. Review the suggested habitat types.
  4. Open Submit observation, confirm or adjust the label, and submit.

How the AI works

  • AI-Hab analyses the whole scene (not individual species) to classify habitat types.
  • Results are based on the UKHab framework, ensuring consistency with professional surveys.
  • The system provides a top prediction plus alternative matches.
  • Each result includes a confidence score to help you judge reliability.
  • You are always responsible for the final selection.

Tips for better results

  • Take photos that show the whole habitat, not just close-up details.
  • Use good lighting — avoid shadows or glare.
  • Keep the image steady and in focus.
  • Make sure the habitat is the main subject of the image.

Understanding results

  • The top result is the AI’s best estimate — not a guaranteed answer.
  • Alternative suggestions help you compare similar habitat types.
  • Confidence values indicate how certain the model is.

Submitting an observation

Required

  • Your full name
  • Habitat label (you can change the AI suggestion)
  • Observation date and time (UTC)

Optional

  • Level 4 UKHab refinement (if available)
  • Your confidence in the selected habitat
  • Notes for additional context

What gets uploaded

  • The photo you provided
  • Observation details: date, time, location (if enabled), AI predictions, your selected label, confidence, and notes
Only upload images you have permission to share.

Map of contributions

  • Open the map to view contributions summarised by OSGB 10km grid squares.
  • The colour of each square reflects how many distinct selected habitat codes were recorded in that 10km grid square.
  • Selecting a square opens details about the distinct habitat codes counted for that 10km grid square.
  • Use the map to track coverage and identify 10km squares that need more survey effort.
